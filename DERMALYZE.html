How the scoring works

When you run a scan, Dermalyze draws your photo to an in-browser canvas and computes four classic dermoscopy heuristics directly from the pixel data — no image ever leaves your device:

  • Asymmetry — mirrors the image and measures pixel-level difference between halves.
  • Border irregularity — runs an edge-gradient (Sobel-style) pass and scores how jagged the lesion boundary is.
  • Color variance — measures spread across the color channels; multiple colors in one lesion is a classic flag.
  • Diameter / coverage — estimates how much of the frame the lesion occupies relative to typical reference sizes.

These four scores are combined into a composite pattern match against five broad, common categories. That match is a statistical pattern-read, not a diagnosis.

Limitations, in plain terms

This build ships with a transparent, rules-based heuristic engine — not a clinically trained deep-learning model. It has not been validated against biopsy-confirmed cases, is sensitive to lighting, focus, skin tone, and camera angle, and cannot see texture, raised borders, itch, pain, or history — all of which matter clinically.

To make this production-grade, swap the scoreImage() function for a call to a real model trained on a labeled clinical dataset (e.g. HAM10000 / ISIC) served behind an API, and have a licensed dermatologist validate its outputs before anyone relies on them.

Please read

Dermalyze is a demonstration tool. It does not diagnose disease, is not a substitute for professional medical advice, and should never delay you from seeing a doctor — especially for a lesion that is new, changing, bleeding, or growing. If something looks concerning, book a dermatologist appointment regardless of what any app tells you.
